FERB Privacy Policy

Last updated: 24 November 2026

FERB is a personal assistant for macOS. It is made of the FERB app, which runs on your Mac, and the FERB browser extension. This policy covers both, and the FERB website you download it from.

What the extension does with your data

The extension connects only to the FERB app on the same Mac, through the browser's native messaging. It has no servers, sends no analytics and runs no code from the internet. It works only in browser profiles you pair with FERB. In those profiles it does three things:

  • Tasks. To carry out a task you asked for, it reads the pages of tabs a FERB task opened or took over (text, accessibility tree, URL and title, and screenshots of that tab) and clicks, types, scrolls, uploads and downloads there.
  • Activity history. It tells the FERB app each time a tab finishes loading a page or becomes the active tab (and when a tab is opened or closed): the page's address (with password, token and similar parameters removed) and its title. FERB keeps the page loads and tab switches in its activity history on your Mac. Private (incognito) windows are never recorded.
  • Hiding secrets in screenshots. When FERB takes a screenshot of a browser window (it does this each time you send a message while that window is in front, and when a task looks at the window), the extension runs its own bundled script in the tab you can see to find where password and one-time-code fields are, so FERB can paint over them. It reads where those fields are, never what's in them, and tells the FERB app that tab's address (with password, token and similar parameters removed) and title, so FERB knows which page the screenshot shows. When it can't (the window's profile isn't paired with FERB, or the page can't be read), the screenshot is still used: password fields macOS Accessibility can see are still painted over, and other secrets are hidden by on-device text recognition, which can miss a secret it doesn't recognise as one.

It passes what it reads to the FERB app, and to nothing else.

What the FERB app does with your data

  • Stored on your Mac. FERB keeps its records in a local database on your Mac: your tasks and what it did, its memory, and the activity it notices (the apps and windows you use, tab addresses and titles, text you type in ordinary fields, text you copy, notifications, apps you install or remove, and changes in folders you asked it to watch). Typed and copied text is kept for 7 days, other activity for 30 days. The app uploads none of this to a FERB server.
  • Sent to the AI provider you connect. FERB sends context to the AI model service you set up in FERB (for example OpenAI through Codex, or Anthropic), under that provider's own terms: to carry out the tasks you ask for, and also to decide whether something it noticed is worth telling you, to write your daily summary and to organize its memory. This can include page text, a redacted screenshot of the window you have in front when you send a message, transcripts of sound FERB listens to during a task, and the parts of your recent activity that relate to your message.
  • Optional triage service. If you add a Jev key, FERB also sends batches of redacted activity (app and window names, tab addresses and titles, typed and copied text, notifications, apps installed or removed, and file paths in folders you asked it to watch) to Jev, a triage service run by typesafe.ai (api.typesafe.ai), which answers whether a moment may be worth telling you about. Without a Jev key nothing is sent there. When Jev says yes, the decision and the message to you are made by your AI provider (above).
  • Speech stays on your Mac. Dictation in the FERB app, push-to-talk and the sound FERB listens to during a task are turned into text by Apple's speech recognition on your Mac only. Where your Mac can't recognize a language on-device, FERB says so instead of sending your voice anywhere. Recorded sound is deleted once it has been turned into text.
  • Secrets are removed first. Before anything is stored or leaves your Mac, FERB removes known kinds of secrets: password fields (also while "show password" is on), one-time codes with a word like "code", "OTP" or "verification" near them, values written right after words like "password", "PIN", "API key" or "token", recognizable API keys and tokens, and card numbers. It works by recognizing these patterns, so a secret written some other way can get through. Passwords and one-time codes you give FERB are filled into pages through a sealed path and are never shown to the AI model.
  • Not sold or shared. Your data is never sold, never used for advertising, never used to judge creditworthiness, and never used for anything except running FERB's features for you: your tasks, the things it tells you about, your daily summary and its memory.

What the FERB website keeps

To download FERB from this website you sign in with Google. For each Google account that signs in, the website keeps exactly this:

  • the account's email address and name, as Google shares them at sign-in;
  • when it first signed in, and when it last downloaded FERB;
  • whether it has unsubscribed from FERB's emails.

Nothing else: Google never shares your password, and nothing from the FERB app or the extension reaches this list. It is used for two things only: to know how many people use FERB, and to email you about important FERB updates (which may include an invitation to talk with FERB's maker about how it works for you). It is never sold, shared or used for advertising. Every email has a one-click unsubscribe; once you unsubscribe, FERB doesn't email you again, even if you sign in again.

The list is kept in a database run by Neon for Vercel, which hosts this website, and emails are sent through Resend. The website sets two short-lived cookies of its own: one for the few minutes a sign-in takes, and one that keeps FERB's maker signed in to see the list. Analytics cookies are set only if you say OK to analytics (below). To have your record deleted, reply to any FERB email or ask through the contact below.

Analytics on this website

This website uses PostHog (posthog.com), an analytics service, to see how people use the website and to fix what doesn't work. It covers this website only: the FERB app and the extension send nothing to PostHog.

PostHog runs only after you press OK on the question at the bottom of the page. Until you answer, and if you choose No thanks, PostHog isn't loaded, nothing is sent and nothing is stored except your answer, which stays in this browser. If your browser sends Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control, the website takes that as No thanks and doesn't ask. The pages for signing in, downloading and unsubscribing, and the list only FERB's maker sees, never load PostHog.

After you press OK, PostHog collects, on this website's pages:

  • What you do on the page: the pages you open and leave, the links and buttons you click (including Continue with Google), where you click, how far you scroll and clicks that do nothing, used for heatmaps and counts.
  • Session recordings: a replay of the page as you use it: what the page shows, mouse movement, scrolling and clicks. Anything typed into a field is masked in the recording and never kept.
  • Speed and errors: how fast the page loads and responds (web vitals), the page's own requests' timings, and errors in the page's code.
  • About your visit: your browser, operating system, device type and screen size, language, the page that sent you here, and your IP address, from which PostHog works out an approximate location.

To count visits from the same browser together, PostHog keeps a random identifier in a cookie and in this browser's storage, both for ferb.kartikey.xyz only. Addresses are stripped of sign-in codes, link tokens and email addresses before anything is sent.

PostHog processes this data for FERB under its own terms and privacy policy, and keeps it for as long as FERB's PostHog project is set to, within the limits of that project's plan. It is used only to improve this website, never sold, shared or used for advertising.

To change your answer, use the button below, or clear this website's data in your browser. Choosing No thanks after OK stops PostHog in this browser and removes its cookie and stored identifier; to have what was already collected deleted, ask through the contact below.

Your control

You choose which browser profiles to pair, and you can remove the extension at any time. You can see everything FERB did with ferb trace and ferb timeline, and you can delete FERB's local data by uninstalling it with ferb uninstall --purge (plain ferb uninstall stops FERB but keeps its data).
