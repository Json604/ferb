FERB Privacy Policy

Last updated: 24 November 2026

FERB is a personal assistant for macOS. It is made of the FERB app, which runs on your Mac, the FERB browser extension, and the FERB apps for iPhone and Android, which work with FERB on your Mac. This policy covers all of them, your FERB account, and the FERB website you download FERB from.

What the extension does with your data

The extension connects only to the FERB app on the same Mac, through the browser's native messaging. It has no servers, sends no analytics and runs no code from the internet. It works only in browser profiles you pair with FERB. In those profiles it does three things:

  • Tasks. To carry out a task you asked for, it reads the pages of tabs a FERB task opened or took over (text, accessibility tree, URL and title, and screenshots of that tab) and clicks, types, scrolls, uploads and downloads there.
  • Activity history. It tells the FERB app each time a tab finishes loading a page or becomes the active tab (and when a tab is opened or closed): the page's address (with password, token and similar parameters removed) and its title. FERB keeps the page loads and tab switches in its activity history on your Mac. Private (incognito) windows are never recorded.
  • Hiding secrets in screenshots. When FERB takes a screenshot of a browser window (it does this each time you send a message while that window is in front, and when a task looks at the window), the extension runs its own bundled script in the tab you can see to find where password and one-time-code fields are, so FERB can paint over them. It reads where those fields are, never what's in them, and tells the FERB app that tab's address (with password, token and similar parameters removed) and title, so FERB knows which page the screenshot shows. When it can't (the window's profile isn't paired with FERB, or the page can't be read), the screenshot is still used: password fields macOS Accessibility can see are still painted over, and other secrets are hidden by on-device text recognition, which can miss a secret it doesn't recognise as one.

It passes what it reads to the FERB app, and to nothing else.

What the FERB app does with your data

  • Stored on your Mac. FERB keeps its records in a local database on your Mac: your tasks and what it did, your chats with it, its memory, and the activity it notices (the apps and windows you use, tab addresses and titles, text you type in ordinary fields, text you copy, notifications, apps you install or remove, and changes in folders you asked it to watch). It also keeps the screenshots it took, copies of files before it changed them, and a record of every action it took and every question it asked you. The app uploads none of this to a FERB server.
  • How long. Text FERB read from apps on screen, screenshots and other evidence, and copies of files kept for undo: 7 days. Typed and copied text: 7 days. Other activity: 30 days. The detailed record of how a task ran: 30 days after it ends. Action and safety records: 90 days. Your tasks, chats, daily summaries and memory stay until you delete them. You can change most of these times in Settings → Privacy.
  • Sent to the model service you connect. FERB thinks with the service you choose in Settings → Thinking: ChatGPT (through Codex), Claude (through Claude Code), or an Anthropic, OpenAI or Google Gemini API key. FERB sends it context, under that service's own terms, to carry out the tasks you ask for, to look over your apps once when you set FERB up, and to organize its memory. This can include page text, a redacted screenshot of the window you have in front when you send a message, transcripts of sound FERB listens to during a task, the parts of your recent activity that relate to your message, and the city your Mac is in (see Location). Your daily summary is written on your Mac without it. Claude Code adds its own text to each request, which includes the email of the Claude account it is signed in with.
  • Location. FERB asks for Location so it knows where your Mac is. It tells the model service your city with each message, never your exact position. Your Wi-Fi network's name is kept on your Mac; FERB can look it up during a task, and it then goes to the model service with that task. Your exact position goes to the model service only for a task that needs it, after you say Yes, once per task. To turn coordinates into a city name, macOS asks Apple's map service.
  • Speech stays on your Mac. Dictation in the FERB app, push-to-talk and the sound FERB listens to during a task are turned into text by Apple's speech recognition on your Mac only. Where your Mac can't recognize a language on-device, FERB says so instead of sending your voice anywhere. Recorded sound is deleted once it has been turned into text.
  • Secrets are removed first. Before anything is stored or leaves your Mac, FERB removes known kinds of secrets: password fields (also while "show password" is on), one-time codes with a word like "code", "OTP" or "verification" near them, values written right after words like "password", "PIN", "API key" or "token", recognizable API keys and tokens, card numbers, and government, tax, bank and health ID numbers it can recognize by their label or check digit. It works by recognizing these patterns, so a secret written some other way can get through. Passwords and one-time codes you give FERB are filled into pages through a sealed path and are never shown to the model service: it gets a stand-in, never the secret.
  • Memory import (off until you turn it on). If you turn it on, FERB reads your last month of conversations with other assistants you choose (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Claude Code, Codex) to learn your preferences. For the websites, FERB asks for your export in your own browser, waits for its email, downloads only the parts it needs, and deletes the file when the import is done. What it reads goes to your model service to be summarized; what it learns is kept in its memory on your Mac, where you can review and remove it.
  • Temporary chat. A temporary chat isn't saved in your chat history and FERB doesn't learn or remember anything from it. The records FERB always keeps for safety (what its tasks did, the questions it asked you, its screenshots) are kept for their usual time.
  • FERB's own email account (optional). If you give FERB its own Google account, the extension reads that account's inbox, in the browser profile you set up for it, when a task needs it.
  • Updates. Once a day FERB asks this website whether there is a newer version; the files come from FERB's public releases on GitHub. The check sends nothing about you beyond what any download sends (the app's version and your network address).
  • Send to developer (only when you press it). When something goes wrong, FERB can show you a report of what happened. Only if you press Send does it go to FERB's maker, as a private report, with secrets removed again first. Nothing is sent by itself, and FERB has no crash reporting or analytics.
  • Not sold or shared. Your data is never sold, never used for advertising, never used to judge creditworthiness, and never used for anything except running FERB's features for you: your tasks, the things it tells you about, your daily summary and its memory.

The FERB phone apps

  • Your phone talks to your Mac directly. The phone app works with FERB on your Mac; it has no model service of its own. Everything between them is end-to-end encrypted. When they can't reach each other directly, a relay passes the encrypted traffic along: your own relay if you set one up, otherwise a public relay run by n0 (the makers of the connection software), which carries only messages, questions and Stop, never files or the live view. A relay can't read what passes through it; it can see the network addresses of your devices.
  • What the phone keeps. Your chat as it fetched it from your Mac, its own keys, and your FERB sign-in, in the phone's secure storage. Your Mac keeps a record of what each paired phone did, for 90 days.
  • The live view and the camera. When you watch FERB work from your phone, or use Show me to look through your Mac's camera while FERB keeps a closed Mac awake, the pictures go straight to your phone and are kept nowhere: not on disk, not in a log, never sent to the model service. They travel only directly or through your own relay. The phone keeps them out of screenshots.
  • Notifications. FERB's notifications come from your Mac over the same encrypted connection; no Apple or Google push service is used. Your lock screen never shows the words of a question about money, deleting something or your account's security.
  • Speaking. When you hold the microphone, your phone turns speech into text on the phone where it can. Where it can't, it says so first and then uses the phone's own speech service (Apple's on iPhone, usually Google's on Android), which may receive your voice. Nothing you say is sent to your Mac as sound.
  • Location (if you turn it on). The phone can tell FERB its city; on Android, turning a position into a city name uses the phone's own map service.
  • Same account to pair. A phone pairs only with a Mac signed in to the same FERB account (see below), and only after you say Yes on your Mac.

What the FERB website keeps

Downloading FERB from this website needs no sign-in, and the website keeps no list of who downloads it. You sign in inside the FERB app instead. If you once signed in here to download FERB, the website still keeps that record (your Google email and name, and when you signed in and last downloaded) in a database run by Neon for Vercel, to know who uses FERB. FERB doesn't send emails. The record is never sold, shared or used for advertising. To have it deleted, ask through the contact below. Analytics cookies are set only if you say OK to analytics (below).

Your FERB account

The FERB apps on your Mac and phone ask you to sign in with Google or Apple, so your Mac and your phone know they belong to the same person. Google and Apple never share your password with FERB. For your account, the website keeps:

  • an account number FERB makes, the email address and name Google or Apple shares (with Apple's Hide My Email this is a private relay address), and whether you signed in with Google, Apple or both;
  • when you first and last signed in;
  • for each device you sign in on: whether it's a Mac, an iPhone or an Android phone, the FERB version, and when it was first and last seen;
  • the sign-in tokens your devices use and the one-time codes that finish a sign-in (they stop working after two minutes), each kept only in a scrambled (hashed) form that can't be turned back into the token or code. Expired ones are removed.

Your account is kept in the website's database, run by Neon for Vercel, so FERB knows who uses it and so you can sign in on your Mac and your phone with the same account. FERB doesn't send emails. Your account is never sold, shared or used for advertising. The website sets one short-lived cookie of its own, for the few minutes a sign-in from a FERB app takes.

On your devices, the sign-in tokens are kept only in secure storage: your Mac's Keychain, the iPhone's Keychain, or storage on Android encrypted with a key the phone keeps. FERB never writes them to a file or a log and never shows them to the model service.

When you pair your phone, the website signs a short note naming your account (its number and email) and that phone, valid for five minutes; your Mac checks it and pairs only if both are signed in to the same account. To protect sign-in from abuse, the website counts requests for a few minutes; network addresses are kept only in a scrambled form, and old counts are removed.

Sign out ends that device's sign-in; on a phone it also unpairs the phone from your Mac. Delete account in any FERB app deletes all of it at once: your account, its devices and its sign-ins. Phones paired with your Mac under an account that is no longer the Mac's are unpaired.

Analytics on this website

This website uses PostHog (posthog.com), an analytics service, to see how people use the website and to fix what doesn't work. It covers this website only: the FERB app and the extension send nothing to PostHog. The list only FERB's maker sees never loads PostHog.

Every visit is counted anonymously. Before you answer the question at the bottom of the page, after No thanks, and when your browser sends Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control (the website then doesn't ask), PostHog counts only: the pages you open and leave and how far down you scroll, clicks on Continue with Google, the page that sent you here, and your browser, operating system, device type, screen size and language. Nothing is stored in your browser for this, nothing is recorded, and nothing names you. To tell one visit from another within a day, PostHog's servers combine your IP address and browser with a random value that changes every day and is deleted after that day; your IP address is removed before anything is kept, so these visits have no location, and the same visitor counts as new each day.

With your OK, PostHog also collects, on this website's pages:

  • What you do on the page: the links and buttons you click, where you click, how far you scroll and clicks that do nothing, used for heatmaps and counts.
  • Session recordings: a replay of the page as you use it: what the page shows, mouse movement, scrolling and clicks. Anything typed into a field is masked in the recording and never kept.
  • Speed and errors: how fast the page loads and responds (web vitals), the page's own requests' timings, and errors in the page's code.
  • About your visit: as above, plus an approximate location PostHog works out from your IP address; the address itself isn't kept.

To count your visits from the same browser together, PostHog then keeps a random identifier in a cookie and in this browser's storage, both for ferb.kartikey.xyz only. Addresses are stripped of sign-in codes, link tokens and email addresses before anything is sent.

PostHog processes this data for FERB under its own terms and privacy policy, and keeps it for as long as FERB's PostHog project is set to, within the limits of that project's plan. It is used only to improve this website, never sold, shared or used for advertising.

To change your answer, use the button below, or clear this website's data in your browser. Choosing No thanks after OK stops the recordings and the rest in this browser and removes PostHog's cookie and stored identifier; the anonymous count goes on. To have what was already collected deleted, ask through the contact below.

Your control

You choose which browser profiles to pair and which phones to pair, and you can remove the extension or a phone at any time. In the FERB app on your Mac, Settings → Stored data shows everything FERB keeps in its database, as stored, and lets you delete a turn of a chat or make FERB forget a memory; Settings → Privacy sets how long records are kept and which apps and websites FERB never records. Settings → Advanced has Uninstall FERB…, which keeps your data, and Uninstall FERB and delete all its data…, which deletes FERB's folder and every FERB item in your Keychain. Settings → Account has Sign out and Delete account.
