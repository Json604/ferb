FERB Privacy Policy

Last updated: 14 October 2026

FERB is an open-source personal assistant for macOS. It is made of the FERB app, which runs on your Mac, and the FERB browser extension. This policy covers both.

What the extension does with your data

The extension connects only to the FERB app on the same Mac, through the browser's native messaging. It has no servers, sends no analytics and runs no code from the internet. It acts only in browser profiles you pair with FERB, and only on tabs a FERB task opened or took over. To carry out a task you asked for, it reads the task's pages (text, accessibility tree, URL and title, and screenshots of that tab) and performs clicks, typing, scrolling, uploads and downloads. It passes what it reads to the FERB app, and to nothing else.

What the FERB app does with your data

  • Stored on your Mac. FERB keeps its records (tasks, what it did, memory) in a local database on your Mac. Nothing is uploaded to a FERB server; there isn't one.
  • Sent to the AI provider you connect. To understand and carry out a task, FERB sends the task's context to the AI model service you set up in FERB (for example OpenAI or Anthropic), under that provider's own terms. This can include page text, screenshots of the window you have in front when you send a message, and transcripts of sound FERB listens to during a task. FERB's background triage model (which decides whether something it observed on your Mac is worth telling you about) also runs at that provider and sees redacted observations.
  • Secrets are removed first. Before anything leaves your Mac, FERB removes known secret types (passwords, one-time codes, API keys, tokens, card numbers). Passwords and one-time codes you give FERB are filled into pages through a sealed path and are never shown to the AI model.
  • Not sold or shared. Your data is never sold, never used for advertising, never used to judge creditworthiness, and never used for anything except the tasks you ask FERB to do.

Your control

You choose which browser profiles to pair, and you can remove the extension at any time. You can see everything FERB did with ferb trace and ferb timeline, and you can delete FERB's local data by uninstalling it with ferb uninstall --purge (plain ferb uninstall stops FERB but keeps its data).
