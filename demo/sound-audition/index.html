FERB sound audition

Throwaway prototype (2026-11-01). Every sound is synthesised live in your browser: modal resonators (inharmonic partials in micro-detuned pairs), a 2–5 ms mallet or a slow rub, an air layer, a small procedural room, gentle saturation, high-pass at 170 Hz. One 3-note motif seeds the whole family; each direction is a different material playing the same notes.

How to judge
  1. Start on the MacBook speakers at a fixed system volume (about 50%). Don't touch it while comparing: every role is loudness-matched across the directions, so differences are timbre and feel, not level.
  2. Use Blind A/B per role (start with Done and Needs you). Pick on gut feel; the tally collects the evidence.
  3. Run the 50-repeat for Done and Needs you in your two favourites. Is it still pleasant at the 40th? Does Needs you still get your attention?
  4. Watch the orb flight with music underneath on. Do the sounds feel like they belong to the orb?
  5. Repeat on AirPods with Panning set to Headphones (HRTF).
  6. Keep the top two; live with each for 2–3 days; re-listen on another day before choosing.

The directions

All sounds

Rows are roles, columns are directions. ▶ plays; WAV saves a 48 kHz / 24-bit file (rendered offline through the same chain, no per-play variation). Frequent sounds vary slightly on every play (±15 cents, ±1.5 dB); the logo never varies.

Blind A/B

Plays the chosen role in two random directions, labelled only 1 and 2. Pick the better one; the answer is revealed and tallied (wins / times heard).

50-repeat

The everyday test: one sound, fifty times, at a steady interval.

Orb flight (direction C of the intro)

The notch swells with the sound logo; the orb drips off, flies to Settings (air follows its speed), lands, the drag succeeds (Done), it hops, flies home and melts back with the swell to the home note. Position maps to pan (full width ≈ ±0.6, notch at centre); distance from the notch adds a small level drop, a low-pass and a little more room.
